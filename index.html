Mexico open data — interactive graphs

Two national datasets seen as networks. The Registro de Emisiones y Transferencias Críticas (RECT, 2004–2022): which states and municipalities report which substances, which ones resemble each other, and how that changes year by year — at national level, and at the level of each of the 32 states individually (pick one from the dropdown on each "State" card). And Mexico's official mortality records (2000–2022, 53.7M entries): which states and municipalities report which causes of death, by ICD chapter or specific code — national level only for now. Every graph opens as an interactive page with a search box, or downloads as PNG, PDF and GEXF (for Gephi). Use the Dataset and Scope filters below to tell them apart.

How to read the graphs

Colors, sizes and connections
  • Blue: states or municipalities. Orange: substances. In the similarity networks, color marks the detected community; in the ego-network, red is the focal municipality.
  • Node size: cumulative emissions 2004–2022 (the sum of every report), almost always on a log scale because totals span more than ten orders of magnitude.
  • Edges: an edge exists when a municipality or state reported that substance. In the similarity networks, when two profiles are alike above a threshold (cosine or Jaccard ≥ 0.7).
What you can do on the interactive pages
  • Tooltip on any node: total and share of the country or state, ranking, years reported, main medium and sector, substances or municipalities with the most kg. Hovering the midpoint of an edge shows the detail of that relationship.
  • Search box (top left): by name or chemical symbol, accent-insensitive. Picking a result zooms in, shows its card and highlights its connections.
  • Buttons and legend: hide or dim a node type (or a community). Dimming also hides its labels and tooltips.
  • On the animated pages: play or drag the year slider; the search and the dim mode are kept when the year changes.
  • Scroll or box-select to zoom; double-click to reset.

Methodology notes

Data and units

The source is Mexico's RECT, with 188,344 reports. Each report carries a kg/year value; the totals shown are the sum of every report from 2004 to 2022, not an annual rate. States, municipalities and substances come from the spatial and interest columns. Some substances appear under several chemical forms (e.g. "Lead (compounds)" and "Lead (soluble compounds)") and are treated as distinct substances.

Methods
  • Similarity between municipalities or states: cosine of the log(1 + kg) vector per substance. Between substances: Jaccard index over the municipalities reporting each one (corrects for very common substances co-occurring by chance).
  • ForceAtlas2 (Gephi Toolkit) with edge-weight influence set to 0 on the bipartite graphs: a handful of huge emitters (Altamira, carbon dioxide) weigh ~1000x more than the rest and collapsed the layout. The similarity networks do use edge weight.
  • Communities: Louvain (Python) or Modularity (Gephi), depending on the graph. Centrality: Gephi's betweenness and PageRank.
  • Maps: approximate municipal-seat coordinates (schematic, not for cartographic use).
Mortality graphs: what's different

Built from Mexico's official mortality records (2000–2022, 53.7M entries after removing every aggregate/placeholder row — no "Total" state, municipality, sex, age bracket or unlabeled cause). Node/edge size is the death count (not a rate: age/sex-specific rates aren't additive, so counts are summed instead), and state/municipality names come from INEGI's official catalog rather than the source file's own text columns, which had corrupted accented characters.

"Cause of death" comes in two granularities, shown as separate graph variants: the 23 ICD chapters (broad categories, e.g. all circulatory diseases together) with no further pruning, or the top 100 specific ICD codes nationally by death count (e.g. "I21 — Acute myocardial infarction") — municipality-level specific-cause edges are additionally pruned to those with >100 deaths accumulated over the period, since the most common causes are reported almost everywhere and would otherwise produce over a million edges. The municipality↔cause graph also has a third, uncapped variant with all 1,511 specific causes and every municipality (568,317 edges) — it draws in WebGL and only shows rich tooltips on its 2,500 heaviest edges, since a static PNG/PDF render at that scale is not practical. No state-level (Tamaulipas-style) mortality graphs exist yet.

Reproduce

Project structure and commands

Each folder holds the scripts and outputs for its graph. Shared modules live at the root: prtr_hover.py (tooltips), prtr_i18n.py (English translations for substances/media/sectors), prtr_ui.py and ui_snippet.js (search box and buttons), and prtr_fa2_html.py. The prtr_raw_for_stori_stori.csv dataset is not version-controlled: place it at the root to regenerate.

pip install networkx plotly matplotlib numpy scipy pillow
python nacional_gephi/nacional_html.py
graphs_pollutants/build_state.sh "Jalisco"         # regenerates all 9 graph types for one state
python build_index.py                             # this page

graphs_pollutants/build_state.sh runs the full per-state pipeline (base GEXFs, ForceAtlas2/Modularity via the Gephi Toolkit, HTML rendering) for any of the 32 states — it's what produced every graph under graphs_pollutants/estados/, including Tamaulipas' own. The Gephi-rendered images (ForceAtlas2, Modularity, PDF/PNG) are produced by the Java programs in each folder together with gephi-toolkit-0.10.1-all.jar. See the project's README.md for details.

The mortality graphs live under graphs_mortality/nacional_gephi/, built by build_mortality_national_gexf.py and mortality_national_html.py from Mexico's raw mortality CSVs plus inegi_catalogo_municipios.csv (a name catalog fetched from INEGI's public geostatistics API), same Gephi Toolkit pipeline as RECT.
